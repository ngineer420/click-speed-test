Five seconds is the sprint distance

Five seconds is the shortest run where a rhythm actually forms. The first click is no longer most of the score, you have time to correct a bad start, and yet the run ends before your forearm has any idea it is working. That combination is why five seconds is the length people reach for when they want to compare two grips, two mice or two techniques against each other.

It is also the most flattering honest length on the site. Nearly everyone scores higher over five seconds than over ten, and higher over ten than over thirty, because fatigue is cumulative and five seconds does not accumulate much of it. If you want the biggest number you can get without changing technique, this is where it lives.

Use it as a control. Run five seconds, change exactly one thing — how you rest your wrist, whether your arm is braced on the desk, which finger you use — and run it again. Over a short distance the difference from a single change is usually visible in two or three attempts.

Questions

What is a good 5 second CPS?

Six to eight is a normal quick clicker. Nine and above over five seconds is fast, and past about eleven you are almost certainly using jitter or butterfly clicking rather than tapping with one finger.

Why do technique comparisons use five seconds?

Because fatigue is the biggest confound in a click test and five seconds barely generates any. Comparing two techniques over sixty seconds mostly measures which one tires you out slower, which is a different question.

Should I warm up first?

Yes, and it matters more here than on the long tests. A cold hand costs most people half a click per second. Two throwaway runs before the one you count is enough.
